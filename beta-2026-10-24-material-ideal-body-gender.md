I think it's helpful to split gender into three separate concepts: material gender, ideal gender, and body gender, frequently conflated and often co-occurring, but fundamentally different. I'll start with material gender.

## Material Gender

Material gender parallels economic class dynamics. The material gender system is an oppressive system that imposes a hierarchical relationship of power dynamics and economic roles. It is built on narratives and stereotypes and expectations. Note that material gender is society-specific, and throughout this piece I'm referring to the society I live in.

For example, the expectation that men are breadwinners and soldiers and protectors and exert power over women and children through sexuality, marriages and families, as heterosexual husbands and fathers. That men's labor is externally visible, and rewarded with the instruments of control, money, authority, and the legitimate use of force within the domain of control, the nuclear family.

The expectation that women are housewives and relegated to reproductive work and care work and domestic work and sexual labor to satisfy a husband and attractiveness labor for heterosexual men and emotional labor. That this labor is rewarded only within the context of submission to control by a man, and rendered utterly dependent on that control.

The expectation that if someone resists, fails, or exits this binary, they are relegated to a gender underclass. Fired from legitimate work. Pushed towards sex work, taboo attractiveness labor, the margins of society. Acceptable targets of bullying and violence, especially corrective violence. This gender underclass is held as a threat hanging over people occupying one of the two legitimate, binary material genders. Don't step out of line or you'll end up like them. Narratively visible only in freakshows, as murder victims on cop dramas, as twisted murders, as punchlines on sitcoms.

This gender underclass is the one that the slurs refer to, reclaimed and unreclaimed. Queer and dyke and f\*\*\*\*t and t\*\*\*\*y and s\*\*\*\*\*e and t\*\*p and f\*\*a and more. Milder insults are threats towards members of the legitimate gender roles: conform or be outcast to the underclass. Gay and metrosexual and soyboy and effeminate and mannish and more. 

### Background on Material Gender

This material gender system is closely based off the gender ternary described by the Sizhen system in two excellent essays,
[Faggotization and the Extant Gender Ternary](https://thesizhensystem.substack.com/p/faggotization-and-the-extant-gender) and
[Gender Ternary and Subalternization](https://thesizhensystem.substack.com/p/gender-ternary-and-subalternization).
 The former is rushed, informal, and broad strokes, while the second is polished, formal, and thought-out in detail. I recommend both, but with the caveat that the first piece is written with a transfem focus, which at times overshadows other queer experiences. The second piece is better in this way, and is also more explicitly political, revolutionary, and gender-abolitionist - you can decide if that's appealing. Note that both pieces only deal with material gender, and reject the concept of ideal gender. I disagree with both pieces in this aspect, but we'll get to that.

### Material Gender Terminology

The pieces, especially the second, introduce some terminology:

* Gender-class, what I've been calling material gender, built of hierarchy, oppression, and power dynamics
* Power, the gender-class at the top of the gender-class hierarchy, the breadwinner, soldier, patriarch, husband (of a wife), father.
* Not-Power, the gender-class subject to Power, legitimate but controlled by Power, the housewife, childcarer, pregnancy-carrier, home cook, home cleaner, mother, wife (of a husband).
* Subaltern, the gender underclass, illegitimate and outcast from society, but still present to use for taboo labor and to threaten the members of the legitimate gender-classes with demotion if they stray, and as a punishment for those who do.

I'll be referring to the gender-classes as Power, Not-Power and either Subaltern or the gender underclass for the rest of the piece, for a good reason: Power is not the same as the category of men, nor cis men, nor straight cis men. Not-Power is not the same thing as being a woman. Subaltern is not the same thing as trans or nonbinary or queer.

### Distinguishing gender-class from standard societal gender

Let's see why the gender-classes are not the same as these more commonly-discussed categories. If you want to be a man, you can just be a man. Trans men are men, and the desire to be a man is plenty to be trans. But if you want to be Power, it's not something you can choose. It's a description of a position in a hierarchy. Of relationships with specific people and with society.

Moreover, the gender-classes are legible, understandable at an intuitive level, to the broad majority of the society that perpetuates them, even if the society doesn't have words for the gender-classes, even if the society doesn't consciously recognize this structure. When I realized I was non-binary, in April 2019, society didn't recognize me as non-binary. Over the years, I told countless hundreds of people what it means to be nonbinary, educated even supportive people on how to use they/them pronouns, and saw people gradually construct a nonbinary category, or try and fail, or not try at all. Nonbinariness is not legible to my society.

The categories of "male", in the sense of bodily sex, and of "assigned male at birth", are likewise not consistently legible to society. Society doesn't know what hormones do or do not do, like the persistent misconception that E raises one's voice to a cis-woman-normative range. Society doesn't know what organs someone has, as shown by the effectiveness of tucking and gender-affirming prosthetics. Endless stories of stealth trans people being told in all sincerity by their transphobic colleagues that they can always tell if someone's trans, while missing the trans person they're talking to. No, these categories too are illegible to society.

And this illegibility exists far beyond trans spaces, as we remember every September 25th when [the following meme](https://www.reddit.com/r/bi_irl/comments/v9vtt3/bi_irl/) is reposted:

> "Today marks a year since I kissed a very attractive twink in Brighton and then found out she was a lesbian who thought I was a lesbian." - Jake Johnstone

It's a meme because it's familiar to many queer people: the categories of "person who looks like an attractive twink" and "person who looks like an attractive lesbian" overlap, they are not instinctively separable, not completely legibly distinct.

In contrast, Subaltern, the gender underclass, is completely legible to my society. When a mass-market porn site has a trans category, they don't need pages of explanation and gender theory. The viewers get it. They don't know if the sex worker they're watching is a man or a woman or something else, but they instinctively know she's Subaltern, underclass. Designated as not worthy of respect, outcast, only eligible to perform taboo labor.

In Silence of the Lambs, Buffalo Bill is the underclass disgusting serial killer that the FBI must stop at all costs, including turning to Hannibal Lecter, the high-class enthralling serial killer that the FBI tentatively allies with. Buffalo Bill (BB) is clearly Subaltern, with no complex theory needed.

Clarise Starling declares that BB is not a genuine transsexual but believes that he is, having been gatekept out of gender-affirming care. The director declared BB a tormented man, while using a backstory describing the experience of thousands of trans women and transfem nonbinary people. This director, this movie, and the society that made it a best seller that swept the Oscars, none of them could recognize a woman, a non-binary person, a man. The movie's depiction of BB draws on all three concepts, and conflates them and confuses them. But the character is clearly Subaltern.

So we understand that gender-classes are completely legible to society, in contrast to the categories of man, woman, nonbinary, which are not.

### Gender-class for Labor Extraction

It's also essential to understand that the gender-class hierarchy is at its core a mechanism of extraction and control of labor. The labor of Not-Power is extracted in this way, pressured and compelled and uncompensated. This is a key concept of feminism.

The labor of Power is extracted as well, even as they are placed atop this hierarchy. In many societies, though not mine, to be Power is to be a soldier. I signed my draft card, I know how this works. If you don't serve in the draft, you're not a "man" (actually, not Power). It's that simple.

In my society, a different, but no less essential labor is required of Power: the labor of enforcing the gender-class system itself. Control of a wife, of a nuclear family, corrective violence, constant monitoring. The rewards of this hierarchy make this position desirable to many in Power, but it is labor nonetheless.

And labor is required of the Subalterns, too. The sex work, but also to receive scorn and threats and violence, and make the legitimate gender-classes feel superior and to not question or stray from the system.

The gender class system is not separable from the economic class structure of the society, which in my case is bourgeois capitalism. Gender-classes are most flexible when they do not threaten the labor extraction process. Not-Power can work outside the home, as long as they still do the domestic, care, and reproductive labor. 

### Gender Abolition

So I think it's pretty clear, at this point, if you're still reading, that the material gender system sucks. I don't like it. Many people don't like it. I want to tear it down, alongside capitalism and the bourgeois class hierarchy. I thus describe myself as a gender abolitionist, as a person who wants to entirely dismantle the gender-class system and free us all from its shackles.

## Something's Missing

But there's something missing in this analysis. Why did I want to be nonbinary, to be trans? Does a material gender analysis describe how my life has gone? Was it purely because I didn't want to do the labor of Power, enforcing the gender-class hierarchy? Did I choose Not-Power, or Subaltern? Am I still Power, and nothing material has changed?

There's something Power about my gender-class experience, my well-paying job, the way students and colleagues instinctively respect me as an authority figure in a way they do not necessarily respect my Not-Power colleagues. There's something Not-Power as well, the way people instinctively use "she" towards me, think of me as a woman or as a doll in the transfem sense, which spans Not-Power and Subaltern, but is best read as Not-Power in context. The way people react when I cry.

But these events, this material gender, none of it answers why I wanted to be trans, to be nonbinary. It wasn't because I wanted any of the Not-Power class features or because I disliked the Power class. And it was a choice, I didn't fail out of Power, wasn't forcibly expelled to Subaltern. I could've performed Power, and I didn't. And material gender, for all its very useful features, can't tell us why.

My desire to be trans and nonbinary, and the desires of vast numbers of trans people, are core parts of gender. No analysis of gender is complete, if it cannot speak to why people want to be trans. So material gender is only part of the analysis of gender.

This is where I disagree with the Sizhen system, by the by. I think the material gender system she described is extremely insightful, but she describes it as all there is to gender, that gender consists solely of material gender. And I disagree.

## Ideal Gender

To understand why I wanted to be trans and nonbinary, and why I chose to be both, and we need a different analysis framework, complementary to material gender. I propose something which I call ideal gender. Note that this is specific to my experiences - for other people, body-gender is a much bigger deal, which we'll get to later.

I often imagine myself living in a utopian post-capitalist, post-class, post-scarcity society. There is no material gender in this society, no hierarchy and no extraction of labor. But there is still gender.

There are still people who like feeling beautiful, in this future. Still people who like feeling strong. People who like feeling feral, graceful, vibrant, androgynous, emotionally intense, trustworthy, and many more. And not just to feel these ways, but to be these ways. To have these experiences.

And these experiences are part of gender, make no mistake. You can't understand the gender Woman without understanding the ideal-gender experience of feeling beautiful, as well as the Not-Power gender-class extraction of domestic labor. You cannot understand the gender Man without the ideal-gender element of strength. You cannot understand the gender Ulzilna (my ideal gender) without understanding the ideal-gender experience of telling a star it looks cute, and feeling a twinkling kinship.

One might think that feeling beautiful and performing attractiveness labor are the same, but this is frequently not so. Being catcalled is attractiveness labor, but it typically makes people feel gross, not beautiful. When I was exploring gender in private as a teenager, I sometimes felt beautiful, but I never did attractiveness labor, because no one ever saw me, I never talked with anyone, never took any pictures.

So these experiences, as elements of gender, the feelings and the ways of being, these are ideal gender. They exist beyond the service of capitalism and class hierarchy, and can persist even as those structures fall.

This ideal gender is why I chose to be trans, chose to be nonbinary. I want to be beautiful, to be graceful, vibrant, emotionally intense, smart, supportive, knowledgeable, cute, soft, authoritative, wise, strong, decisive, caring, and to tell the stars they're cute. Those are just a fraction of the glittering cloud that is my ideal gender, which I name Ulzilna. And I strongly suspect that ideal gender is a big part of why a lot of trans people have wanted to be trans. Another big reason is body-gender, discussed later in this piece.

### Exploring Ideal Gender

In contrast to material gender, which exists broadly across a society, ideal gender often arises in smaller groups and communities. Every day, someone coins a new ideal-gender term for a new family of ideal-gender experiences, seeking to understand their ideal-gender self. I coined Ulzilna for this reason, as well as transchromatic. The MOGAI group on Tumblr in the mid 2010s popularized this approach to ideal gender. Gender connections to plants and void and monstrousness and air.

Note that just as ideal-gender is experienced personally or in small communities, not everyone experiences it. As far as I can tell from talking with people, agender people don't experience ideal gender. Maybe they have the above desires, but they don't feel associated with gender, or maybe they don't care. Both are fine.

Ideal gender is fundamentally nonhierarchical. If a gender experience involves control, extraction, domination, it might be material gender. Or it might be kink. Kink done well separates the narratives of control, extraction and domination from the realities, allowing them to be safely and consensually explored, moving those narratives into the realm of ideal gender while leaving the realities behind. Kink done poorly just reperpetuates material gender, like unnegotiated choking by a Power of a Not-Power, like domestic control of a Subaltern by a Power in a position of economic and societal power.

## Examples to Distinguish Ideal Gender and Material Gender

It's essential that we don't confuse ideal gender and material gender, because they're very different things. Let's go through a few test cases, in the forms of common slogans around gender and transness, both supportive and nasty.

> "How many genders are there?"

It depends on the kind of gender. There are three gender-classes, Power, Not-Power, and Subaltern (in my society). Subaltern is not subdivided further because subdivisions aren't legible, and because further subdivisions aren't needed to maintain the stable extractive structure.

By contrast, there are millions of ideal-genders, maybe billions, and quite possibly an infinite variety. Every time someone like me coins something like Ulzilna, there's another ideal-gender. Plausibly, everyone's ideal-gender is at least a little different. And perhaps ideal-genders can be said to exist even if they have not yet been embodied or experienced?

So without establishing this separation between material gender and ideal gender, perhaps the most accurate possible answer to the question is "There are at least three." And when asked to name them, without this separation and analysis, perhaps the best answer that can be given is "Don't play games with me, kid."

> "Trans women are women. Trans men are men."

What does this statement mean? Many accounts of the groups "trans women" and "women" make this definitionally true, and thus convey nothing, and a material gender perspective would certainly not say "all trans women are Not Power". 

From an ideal gender perspective, it has more meaning and makes more sense. The phrase "trans women" is here understood to mean "people who want to be women, who were not accepted as women from birth". It is in large part an ideal gender experience, the desire of the set of experiences typically associated with the ideal-gender side of Woman. This identification between "trans women" or "trans men" and people who have a certain ideal-gender is encapsulated by the saying "If you want to be a man, you can just be a man".

And the second use of the word "women" is here understood to refer to the social category of women. Thus we can expand out this quote as "Anyone whose ideal gender aligns with the ideal-gender side of Woman belongs in the social category of Woman". And that's something real, that's something meaningful.

It's advocating to center this socially meaningful category around ideal gender, an experience which is solely internal by default. It implies communication about ideal gender. Are you trans? Do you want to be? Great, you're welcome in our social category. 

Of course, it's only partially achievable. One can only be in a social category if society can discover the quality around which the category is formed. Many people aren't very in touch with their ideal-gender, often because they're suppressing it due to societal pressure, and they will probably not be included in this social category. Likewise closeted people.

To apply this concept well, we must separate our understanding of ideal-gender and gender-class. Otherwise, we might believe incorrect things, like "Everyone whose ideal-gender aligns with ideal-Woman is Not Power". This is clearly wrong. There are many such people who are Power, and many who are Subaltern. One's ideal gender doesn't at all affect one's material gender directly. Each is separately essential to understanding gender.

> "All trans people are valid"

Again, without a concept of ideal gender, this is incomprehensible. What does it mean to be valid, in the frame of material gender?  Nothing. Validity isn't a material condition. It doesn't say anything about forces and pressures and conditions and economic relations. But through the lens of ideal gender, it is clear. It means "Ideal gender is unlimited". Ideal gender can take infinite forms, and all of them exist as ideal genders, without hierarchy. No particular kind of ideal gender is needed to occupy the social category of trans.

## Dangers of Conflating Material Gender and Ideal Gender

Conflating ideal-gender and material gender can have really bad effects. For example, when someone's egg is newly hatched and they ask "how can I be a girl?", they might be referring to body or material issues - how do I get hormones, escape the control of unaccepting parents, build a support network, find work where I can be out. But someone might respond ideally - "If you want to be a girl, you can just be a girl". Which is not helpful, and reinforces the individualistic trap of my society.

In fact, the conflation of ideal-gender and gender-class frequently serves to reinforce the existing hierarchical gender-class system. Oh, you want to be beautiful? Find a boyfriend and submit. You want to be strong? Then you must want to dominate others. Take up a violent sport or join the military. You want society to never perceive you? Straight to the underclass.

The gender-class system uses ideal-gender to block attempts at gender abolition. "They're not gonna let you call yourself a woman or wear dresses anymore! Be afraid! They're gonna take away your guns and make you eat soy! Be afraid!". And in my opinion misguidedly, some people who want to abolish material gender also want to abolish ideal gender, want everyone to have equal propensity for all preferences and interests and all of that. And more power to everyone who wants an absence of ideal gender for themselves, but I don't want that. I really don't want that.

I transitioned for the ideal-gender. I won't give it up. I don't care that someone thinks that when I get cute plushes and take selfies and engage with ideal gender that in doing so I'm upholding gender-class, and I don't care what material-gender argument people make towards that conclusion, I don't find it convincing.

I know why they advocate getting rid of both, because they see them as inextricably intertwined. But I disagree wholeheartedly. I see material gender growing increasingly separate from ideal gender, crumbling with the fall of the bourgeois capitalist class structure that it upholds. And leaving behind ideal-gender, intact. Because no part of my ideal gender requires or even benefits from the maintenance of capitalism. So the elimination of my ideal gender cannot be necessary for its downfall. Whatever our disagreements, I am entirely aligned with the core aim of gender abolitionism, tearing down the edifice of material gender.

## Queer People Frequently Disrupt the Gender-Class Hierarchy

Men who date men, who marry men, who build their lives accordingly, often exist in opposition to the gender-class of Power. They have the opportunity to obtain patriarchal power over a wife and a nuclear family, and often reject that power, building symmetrical relationships with an equal power dynamic. Women who date women, who marry women, who build their lives accordingly, often do the same.

The gender-class system has only one place for such people: Subaltern. Outcast. Underclass. And many men who build their lives with men, and many women who build their lives with women, resist this placement, this ejection, with varying degrees of success. Every time they resist, whether they succeed or fail, the gender-class system grows a little wobblier, we move slightly towards dismantling that hierarchy. Especially if that rejection of Power and Not-Power extends beyond the two of them to their interactions with society.

Note that this is a different category of people than gay or lesbian people. A gay man can occupy Power, if he maintains a heterosexual relationship in line with Power, and only has gay hookups on the side, as many conservative politicians are known for. Likewise, many lesbian women remain stuck in Not-Power for years, decades, or even a lifetime.

On the flipside, bi people and ace people frequently have these types of relationships that disrupt gender-class, these types of lives. Some straight people do as well, like women who thought of themselves as straight when they married someone who they thought of as their husband, who they stayed with when that person came out as a woman, and who together created a more egalitarian relationship.

Relationships between a man and a woman have the potential to buck the material gender structure too, like stay-at-home dads, but it's harder and rarer because the pressure of the material gender system is more acute.

## Something's Still Missing: Body Gender

Material gender and ideal gender are great and all, but bodies are gendered, and that's not something that either of those analyses understand, except at a distance.

Body gender is about how we perceive, prefer, and desire our bodies to be, and how society does the same.

Gendered body features include:
* Muscles - how much, how visible
* Fat distribution
* Hygiene, body cleaning 
* Body odor
* Voice - pitch, buzziness, resonance, volume
* Scars
* Eyes
* Head hair - texture, length, color
* Eyebrows - thickness, central connection
* Facial hair - texture, length, color
* Body hair - texture, length, color
* Skin - smoothness
* Height, size
* Hands - size, width
* Nails - length, opacity, color
* Facial expressions - dimples, resting bitch face
* Breasts 
* Belly - hanging forward vs side, stretch marks
* Genitals
* Reproductive system
* Butt 
* Hips
* Thighs
* Calves
* Feet - size, wearing heels
* And more

Note that this covers primary sex characteristics, secondary sex characteristics, and many other body features that are culturally gendered. Body gender is built on top of sex, but it doesn't end there.

Even the underlying feature, sex, is a socially constructed categorization and narrative on top of the underlying sex-associated biological characteristics like hormone levels, the extent to which hormone receptors are activated, whether hormone levels are constant or fluctuating, presence and functioning of sex-associated organs, chromosomes, and the biological processes downstream of all of these factors.

But plenty of gendered body features are not appreciably sex associated. Resting bitch face does not arise in the hormones, it is a cultural construction around the expected factual features of people societally seen as women. Plenty of people have thick leg hair regardless of hormone levels, but only some people are expected to shave it off. 

These gendered body features are often intentionally shaped, there are intense internal feelings towards them, and intense social pressures upon them. The pathways of gender body shaping include: 
* Skincare,
* Haircare,
* Haircuts,
* Shaving legs,
* Plucking eyebrows,
* Exercise,
* Weight lifting in particular,
* Medications,
* Deodorant,
* Diets,
* Voice training,
* Laser hair removal & electrolysis,
* Hormones,
* Gendered surgeries:
  * hair transplants,
  * fat redistribution,
  * facial cosmetic surgery,
  * bone shaping
  * adding, removing, and resizing, gendered organs
* etc. 

And body gender doesn't end at the skin, doesn't end with one's biological mass. Anything that feels like "oneself" in a gendered way is part of body gender.
* Makeup 
* Nail polish
* Tattoos
* Piercings
* Gendered prosthetics:
  * Wigs & tupees
  * breast forms & binders 
  * packing & tucking
* And more

Clothing, accessories, and jewelry are complicated, because they influence how others perceive us more than how we perceive ourselves. But they are definitely a part of body-gender. Clothes emphasize or conceal body features, as well as convey body gender messages of their own.

Body gender is extremely broad, and it exists without need for categorization and analysis. Any combination of gendered body features and elements of oneself is body gender. Body gender is societally influenced, in the selection of what body parts are considered gendered and what those parts mean.

Some aspects of body gender exist without need for a society, onto which society accretes the rest of the system. Chromosomes are correlated with hormones are correlated with some body features having existed are correlated with some body features existing now. But the tightness of those correlations is extremely societally dependent, as I and many other trans people demonstrate. And those correlations were never all that reliable, as intersex people throughout history demonstrate.

Finally, there is no separation between the body and the mind. The brain receives hormones, like any other. Some aspects of mental functioning, such as the emotions we feel easily and the intensity of those emotions, are best understood by at least partially considering body gender. Exogenous hormones affect that body-gender aspect of the mind quite substantially, to the point where I sometimes describe their mental effects as their primary impact, and everything that's externally visible as secondary.

### Snap gender judgement

When people look at people or hear them, we almost always make a gender judgment, placing the person into one of a handful of gender categories, and often into a smaller subcategory. The top-level category might be man, woman, queer (or nonbinary), and subcategories might include frat bro, gun bro, tech bro, church lady, butch, femme, bear, twink, transfem, transmasc, androgynous, mom, dad, grandma, grandpa, etc.

These snap gender judgments are mostly based off of body gender. Someone sees my dress and they guess woman, they hear my (untrained) voice and they guess man, they perceive my nonnormative variety of features and guess queer.

Note that people might or might not have a nonbinary top-level category. When they don't, it frequently leads to nonbinariness being treated as a subcategory, lumping all of us into boy nonbinary and girl nonbinary, or transfem nonbinary and transmasc nonbinary, or whatever. Different people have different categorization systems, and they can be changed, but they are heavily societally influenced.

It's not a coincidence that the top-level judgements frequently match the material genders in our society. Material gender is built on the bulk of society making these snap judgements and applying material pressures accordingly. Material gender can only apply pressure to the extent that gender is legible, and snap judgements are the way that society reads that gender into a person.

Sometimes, features of material gender outside of body gender might be taken into account during this snap judgement. An ambiguous person carrying a baby might be gendered as a woman. An ambiguous person in a soldier's uniform might be gendered as a man. A street sex worker will often be gendered as queer, regardless of body appearance. But body gender typically overrides these material-only signals: a person with a beard holding a baby will almost always be read as a man. 

Note that snap gendering is not the same thing as material gender. Snap gendering is pretty malleable - change clothes, makeup, wig and/or facial hair, and snap gendering will often change. Material gender is much harder to move. Also for many trans people, we experience many contradictory snap genderings over the course of a day. Material gender is more of a sustained pressure. Finally, body gender is only weakly correlated with economic class, whereas Subaltern is extremely connected with the economic underclass.

In other situations, ideal gender is incorporated into a snap gender judgment. Encountering someone's name or pronouns before perceiving their body. Practicing thinking of someone a certain way, and noticing the features associated with that concept. I remember hearing a podcaster's name as Alex and hearing the voice and snap gendering as a man, then backing up to double check, hearing the name Alice, and suddenly perceiving her voice as more feminine. But usually, body gender overrides ideal gender. Way too many people have heard my voice over the phone and said "Hello, Mr. Grosof, would you like to schedule an appointment for gender affirming care?" or similar for me to think otherwise.

### Disentangling passing

Passing refers to two different concepts, visit related but slightly different. One is about snap gender judgements, mostly based off body gender. Does people's snap gender judgement match the person's ideal gender or preferred top level categorization? The other is relative to conscious examination.

A trans woman is walking down the street, and a person sees her dress and heels and immediately thinks "There's a woman". Then, that person looks at her more closely and sees her receiving hairline and her height and revokes the assessment. This is passing relative to snap judgment but not conscious examination. The reverse is also common.

In transphobic societies, conscious-passing is often held up in trans circles as a pathway to safety and/or access to one of the legitimate gender-classes, Power and Not-Power. Snap-passing is relevant as well, as snap judgements are the mechanism through which material gender is frequently exerted, though one of the more dangerous situations one can be in is snap-passing but not conscious-passing. In such situations, members of Power and Not-Power often take offense to the perceived attempt to access a legitimate gender-class by a Subaltern, met by corrective violence. This is the genesis of the "trans panic" legal defense, though it is frequently used as cover for the more fundamental argument "who cares if I murdered them? They're underclass, no legitimate person was harmed."

Snap-passing is often a goal for many people, outside of trying to move material gender. Many openly trans people love the affirmation that comes with a stranger's first reaction being accurate. This is easier for people who want to be perceived as men or women, and harder for people who want to be perceived as nonbinary, like me.

One important subcategory is self-snap-passing, seeing a boy/girl/queer weirdo in the mirror. I remember the first time I self-snap-passed. It was in the self-view of a Zoom call in late 2023, because the blur of the video compression modified my body-gender by softening my features. I lost track of what was happening in the meeting and started to cry with joy. It was a magical experience, and deeply meaningful despite the odd circumstances.

So we see that passing is an amalgamation of several different concepts, different motivations, and different aspects of the gender system.

### Dysphoria and euphoria

The best guide to gender dysphoria and euphoria I know is [genderdysphoria.fyi](genderdysphoria.fyi), also known as the gender dysphoria bible. Many forms of gender dysphoria that I experience arise through body dysphoria. Facial hair, body odor, balding, chest hair, muscle craginess, arm hair, bottom dysphoria, voice, emotional emptiness, and more. And the euphoria I experience is mostly through body gender as well. Breasts, smooth skin, smile, stretch marks, rounding hips and butt, nails that grow and crack stochastically, and more.

That's not to say that dysphoria and euphoria can't apply to material and ideal gender. Being crushed into the gender-class position of a subservient, controlled spouse in Not-Power makes many people dysphoric, as well as pissing off even more people in general. The freedom and transgression of Subaltern can be euphoric, even as it is deeply unstable and tenuous. On the ideal-gender side, when I first called myself they, it brought me to tears with joy. My feelings are best summarized by FLASCH's song One of Them. The euphoria of ideal gender is the core of the reason I transitioned.

But the framework of gender dysphoria as popularized by the post-WWII American and European medical establishment is grounded firmly in body gender. These doctors saw themselves as experts in diagnosing and fixing bodies, and so saw gender through the lens of body gender. Note however that the medical gatekeeping of the time, which is still ongoing, placed additional material-gender constrictions on trans people. Trans woman were only considered legitimate to the extent that they performed Not Power, found a straight(-performing) husband and did feminine work and had feminine hobbies and so forth. Only people who performed gender-class legibly and conformingly earned the privilege of affirmation of their preferred body gender.

The body-gender-focused attitude persists in the world of medical training to the extent that an endocrinologist I saw in 2024, fresh out of education, learned for the first time from me that hormones have positive mental effects for many trans people. Her training had only covered non-mental body effects and negative mental effects, due to this narrow focus on the treatment of bodily dysphoria.

### An outdated body-material relationship

Body gender and material gender overlap in that they both deal with what exists in the here and now, people's direct experiences and their interactions with society. But material gender is grounded in economic class, and body gender is grounded in sex, and sex is grounded in direct bodily processes.

This grounding doesn't really mean much in the day-to-day, however. Every Not-Power who resigned a career to raise the kids and then could never re-enter the economic position she previously held knows that economic class and material gender have important differences. Every drag queen knows that body gender need not resemble biological processes.

There's a strong argument that our current material gender system is a hold-over from body-gender and sex experiences of pre-industrial peasant labor, a type of economic specialization suited for the reproduction of that society despite low fractions of pregnancies resulting in live births and low fractions of newborns surviving childhood, resulting in very high demand for reproductive labor. To be clear, many people in these societies had no interest in these paths, but the first imperative of many societies is to reproduce themselves - and the ones that don't, cease to exist.

With the high demand for reproductive labor, spanning much of many people's adult lives, there arose a specialization of the labor tasks that could be done while simultaneously performing reproductive labor (pregnancy, breastfeeding, etc.) as "women's work", such as child care, fiber crafts, cooking, cleaning, elder care, sick care, etc. Whereas "men's work" specialized to focus on labor that could not be performed simultaneously with reproductive labor, such as high-muscular-exertion agricultural labor and certain military labor. There's even evidence that the material gender split was far less pronounced before agriculture and state-based large-scale warfare. In the pre-industrial agriculture-heavy era, people were bound into the gender-class system both by the threat of Subalternization and by the economic hardship of leaving the system of economic specialization.

I'm not well-versed enough in history and anthropology to evaluate the details of this argument, and certainly our material-class system has changed to include the idea of a nuclear family.

But there is a real sense in which our gender-class system is ill-suited to our modern economic realities, especially in globally-wealthy societies such as the US. The fraction of "high-muscular-exertion" labor is rapidly diminishing, and universal military drafts are rarer, weakening the economic underpinning of Power. The fraction of work which cannot be simultaneously performed with reproductive labor is falling rapidly, weakening the underpinning of Not-Power. There is no inherent economic difficulty faced by a gay or lesbian couple except for the imposed threat of Subalternization. Many trans people don't need to retrain for a completely different type of labor when they transition, or only for a fraction of their labor. The gender-class is increasingly artificial, imposed, adrift.

And it's no coincidence that more people than ever are leaving the Power / Not-Power pair of legitimate gender-classes. More people than ever are coming out as gay, lesbian, bi, trans, ace, and living their lives authentically, outside of cis-het gender-class system. More people are openly and visibly expressing those gender and sexuality experiences. Polyamory is on the rise.

Because the threat of Subalternization is beginning to fail. People aren't cast out as reliably as they once were - I haven't been Subalternized, for instance, but I likely would've been had I taken a similar path decades ago. Moreover, Subalternization is more survivable than it used to be. Make no mistake, many, many people are still Subalternized every day, and the gender-class oppression is still awful. But the community structures of mutual aid by which Subalternized people stick together and build lives are making the threat not quite as threatening. There's more gender-class traitors (like me) who have solidarity and shared purpose with Subaltern people. There's a path, a life, and a future on the other side.

### Ideal body-gender

Ideal gender lies in the land of dreams, hopes, and wishes, and the stories we tell ourselves and others. Body gender begins with the physical, grounded, extant reality. But it doesn't end there. Body-gender hopes and dreams, the gendered elements of the body we imagine when we close our eyes and turn off the critical part of our minds, that's body gender too. We can call that portion of the experience "ideal body gender".

I've had an ideal body gender image that did not lie within the realm acceptable to my assigned gender for a very long time. Dreams of smooth skin and curvy hips and soft facial features and much more. Of having a body that I could see as beautiful. 

These dreams felt impossible, unrealistic, outside the realm of what was physically possible and achievable. Me, a kid who couldn't wash myself regularly, who couldn't brush or comb my hair, whose embodied body gender was so clearly "boy", how could these dreams be anything but fantasy, and shameful, gross fantasy at that? 

But look at me now: recent photos. My skin is the texture I wished it was, insofar as I had a specific wish. My curves aren't done growing, but they're coming along. My face is soft, not as I would've imagined it but it's moved. I am beautiful, my body is beautiful, though it took time and conversations with my partners and others to help me see that.

I wish I could talk to my old self at 13 or 15, and hug them and tell them that the future is bright, that your dreams will come true. I can't, not directly, but I can talk to the part of myself that is still scared and lonely and ashamed. And I can talk to you.

To be clear, not all aspects of ideal body gender are worth trying to bring into reality. I grew up in a society, a culture, and a media environment that promoted slimness as a key aspect of female body gender. And I was not immune to propaganda. My ideal body gender, when I was young and for many years afterwards, was slim in a way that I'll never be, and that I don't want anymore. It was built off of images of models and seeing classmates and hearing narratives of health and beauty, and I don't endorse those narratives, with what I know now. Of course, these societal processes shape and influence everyone's ideal body gender, whether we engage with or reject those ideals.

This "slim femininity" aspect of my former ideal body gender got in the way of my gender exploration for years, and especially my body gender. When I started trying out feminine clothing, I constantly tried on clothes that were too small for the body I had, trying to squeeze myself smaller. Today I understand the mechanism, and I see my experience shared in many people who have feminine elements of their ideal body genders, whether cis or trans or otherwise. 

But in those days, I popped a seam or two in changing rooms and felt so gross, so alone. In this, too, I wish I could talk to my younger self, show them beautiful, aspirational people who are the same size that I was, that I am. That the core essence of my dreams was authentic, long-term, and achievable even if the manifestation at the time was more transitory.

Bodily transition must be understood not only as a direct response to a negative body-gender experience, the relief of bodily gender dysphoria, but also as a movement towards an ideal body-gender, the creation of something otherwise only imagined. Pulling a dream into one's reality.

### Cis-by-default

Not everyone has an ideal body-gender. Some people are fine with the body they have, and if they woke up one day with a different body, they'd be fine with that too. They have no desire to change their body from its default settings, and also no desire to keep it as it is. The vast majority of such people are cis, and I would term these people "cis-by-default". They are cis because it's easier to be cis, because it's the default, because they have no particular preference to be anything else. Some people have body preferences, even body ideals, but those body preferences aren't seen through a gendered lens. This is another manifestation of an absence of ideal body-gender, and likewise frequently appears among cis-by-default people.

Many cis-by-default people are quite gender-nonconforming, because gender narratives and societal pressures often shape people's gender expression by shaping their ideal gender and their ideal body-gender in particular. People who don't experience much ideal body-gender, people who are cis because it's easiest, often engage less with those pressures and pathways of expression. Cis-by-default women often have short hair, rarely use makeup, or don't remove facial and body hair to the extent to which society demands. Cis-by-default men often have less restricted hobbies and ranges of emotional expression that society demands. Many cis-by-default people have romantic and sexual preferences outside of the heterosexual guidelines of society. None of these are hard-and-fast rules, they're just patterns.

For people with an intense sense of their ideal body-gender, including trans people like me, the experience of cis-by-default people can be hard to wrap one's mind around. My ideal body-gender is a major part of my decision making, day in and day out, so it can be hard to mentally simulate a very different decision-making process. People sometimes invent convoluted motivations for why someone might conceal their ideal body-gender, because the alternative, that their mind functions differently, can be hard to accept.

The reverse is true as well. For people with very little ideal body-gender, like cis-by-default people, the experience of trans people can be hard to envision. They don't even have an ideal body-gender, and now someone's saying that this experience is a major part of their life choices? Seems implausible. And again, people sometimes invent convoluted motivations for why someone might invent the construct of an ideal body-gender out of whole cloth, because the alternative is again hard to accept.

For all this confusion and divergent experiences and poor communication, trans people and cis-by-default people are natural allies. We are all constrained and harmed and oppressed by the coercive system of gender pressures emitted by society, no matter how we navigate that oppression. It reduces our options, sets up pitfalls to ensnare us, and punishes us when we stray.

Moreover, we are on a continuum. The experiences of nonbinary people who don't desire bodily transition and the experiences of cis-by-default people have a lot in common. Different responses to similar underlying experiences.

Let us join together, in organizing and action, to resist the coercive gender pressures that are harming all of us. Let us not be divided by our differing gender experiences and our differing response to that pressure. I will stand with you if you will stand with me.

### Body Gender Liberation

Our body gender should be ours to choose. Our hormones, our organs, our gendered prosthetics, our decorations. No one should have to pass a bar of dysphoria or diagnosis or convincing a doctor. No maze of referrals and second opinions and letters and insurance and clinics and endless waitlists and, and, and. Our bodies are ours, they don't belong to the doctors or the government or anybody else. I'm not saying that people should be left adrift to make those choices. I'm saying that at the end of the day, each of us has the right to choose our body gender for ourself.

And yes, people will sometimes choose a body gender, and later decide that they want another body gender, or to go back to where they started. To make that attempt, to try out that choice, is their right. To change again, to return to start, is their right again. Society may provide information, communities may share experiences, but at the end of the day body liberation is a fundamental human right, and body gender liberation is a key aspect of that broader right.

The fight for body gender liberation is a shared fight alongside everyone else who is fighting for body liberation. The fight for the right to begin and end pregnancies. For disability assistive devices and accommodations.

And body gender liberation isn't just for trans people. Hormone replacement therapy was developed for cis people, especially with regards to hormone levels diminishing as one ages. The first exogenous hormone-affecting medication I took was a DHT blocker marketed to cis men who wanted to stave off balding. Gender affirming surgeries abound for both cis and trans people. Laser and electrolysis hair removal are frequent for both groups. That's liberation: everyone should be able to shape their body to the extent they desire. Gatekeepers and economics and societal pressures shouldn't interfere.

## Conclusion

Gender can only be understood through the lenses of material, ideal, and body gender. You cannot understand how gender oppresses people without understanding material gender. You can't understand how it benefits and enhances people's lives without understanding ideal gender. And you can't understand what it has to do with people's bodies without body gender. These analyses are compatible. We can understand both at once. We don't need to conflate them. Abolish material gender, widen ideal gender, liberate body gender. The future is bright and full of stars.
