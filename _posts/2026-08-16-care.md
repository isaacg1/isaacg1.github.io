The piece is a follow-up to [ADHD and Avoidance](/2026/07/15/adhd-and-avoidance),
which I now realize had a lot to do with avoidance and the spiral of the void,
and not much to do with ADHD except as a trigger for the spiral.

Is this fiction? Nonfiction? Prediction? Manifestation? Sure.

## Care

"Hey, sweetie?"

Ey looked down at me from above, eir wavy hair draped around her face.

I flinched, scrambling to my knees, to my chair as quick as I could.

Ey caught me, held me, hugged me.

"It's Ok, darling, I know what it looks like when you've fallen into the void."

I'd been lying on the rainbow pillow on the floor of my office, playing a pointless puzzle game on my phone. For hours.

I started crying.

How could I squander everything that was unfairly given to me, this beautiful office, the support of my colleagues and my friends, the support of my enfriend, just throwing it all down the drain because I couldn't just squeeze out a few more paragraphs. Why was I like this?

Ey pulled another chair next to mine, swiveling me around to rest my head on eir lap.

"It's Ok, sweetie, I know how you must feel."

It was soft in eir lap, pleated fabric and gentle hands. I wanted to curl into a ball, to push eir away, to do anything to feel less vulnerable. I was so disgusted with myself. But I couldn't move, couldn't do anything that might hurt em.

"You're doing good, just keep crying, that's what you need right now."

Worthless. Worthless and hopeless. I'll never finish this project, never even make appreciable progress on it. I'm gonna have to find a different line of work, but even that won't help because I'll always be like this, always be so fucking useless.

"The first step is feeling your emotions, good, good, good."

She handed me the box of tissues. I wiped my nose, my eyes, and handed the tissue back to her, wincing at making her deal with my effluent.

"Good, now can you give me some deep breaths, baby?"

In, two, three, four. Hold, two, three, four. Out, two, three, four. And again. And again.

The anger and the despair drained out of me, and there was nothing left. The cold air of the office, the bright colors on the walls, the tickle of her around around my head. None of it meant anything. Because I'd never change.

I thought back to the list of emotions I'd made. FAHDSED. Frustration, Anger, Hopelessness, Despair, Shame, Emptiness, Dissociation. Yeah. Yeah, that was what I was feeling.

"Why don't you hate me?", I asked up into the softness.

I felt eir laugh more than heard it.

"You're very silly."

I started to object, before ey continued.

"This is so normal for you, and you're still surprised every time. It's very silly."

More tears, and I curled myself into em, hugging my arms around eir waist.

"They start a big, important project, and after the initial wave of motivation and clear progress are done, they're lying on the floor, desperately washing away the fear and guilt, hour by hour and puzzle by puzzle. You're so, so predictable."

I wanted to push back, to tell em it wasn't like that, but -- I didn't want to lie to em, either.

I sighed, long and heavy.

"I'm sorry -- for, for -- I'm so sorry."

I grimaced as the words came out. The crying had sealed my nose, made my throat big and hollow, given it that quality I hate.

"Rejected."

Ey gave me a little squeeze.

"I don't want you apologize for how you feel. Your shame isn't helping anyone, so don't offer it up to me."

I nodded again. There was more I wanted to say, wanted to protest, but nothing that was worth that sound from my throat. And none of it needed to be said, anyways.

Ey reached to the side, and I heard the spray of water into my wide ceramic mug. It hadn't been easy carrying that 20 pound water jug over from downtown, but it meant I was always prepared. Or we were prepared, I guess, because there's no way I would've thought of that on my own.

She pressed the mug to my lips and I opened to sip at it, as she carefully tilted it and kept the water flowing into me.

When my sips slowed, ey pulled the mug away, wiped at my face with a paper towel, then handed me an apple from the basket next to the water. Green, granny smith, my favorite. Prepared, again.

Because this is normal. I did see it coming. I do know myself.

I had to shift slightly to get a good angle to eat the apple in eir lap, but it was worth it. The food grounded me, helped bring me back to my body. Bite and bite and bite, chew and taste and swallow, nothing left but seeds and stem. I'd lost all track of time and place, in the void.

Ey held eir hand to my face and I spat the seeds in, as eir other hand stroked my hair.

"That's it, such a good little thing you are."

I could breathe a bit easier. Feel safe again. My throat was opening up, felt Ok to talk again.

"Thanks, I -- Thanks."

"Of course, sweetie. Now, let's talk through what we know about coping mechanisms, Ok?"

Another deep breath. Ok. We're doing this.

I sat up, took a moment to let my head settle, looking close to em but just to the side of eir head.

"So, my emotions are real, the whole fahdsed combination."

Ey nodded encouragingly, holding one of my hands.

"And the void, on the floor and on the phone, is a response to those real emptions. I do it for a reason."

Ey reached up to give my head a pat for that. It was hard to admit that the void was a legitimate part of myself, coming from within me rather than some external intrusion.

"But the void is not a helpful response to those emotions, because it just builds a reinforcing spiral."

"Good! And instead?"

"Instead, emotional openness," My eyes flicked to eirs at that, giving em a smile. "Openness, processing, and building comfort with indirect progress."

"That's right, sweetie. And what does that look like?"

I rotated to turn on my machine, holding one of eir hands even if it meant twisting my arm to do so. It took an extra moment to log in one-handed, but it was worth it.

I pulled up the LaTeX draft, where it had devolved into bracketed comments of an increasingly self-recriminating nature.

"A forest grows from layers of fallen trees and decomposing leaves. I can't write a tree that will stand yet, but I can write something that will fall over."

"Very good, sweetie! Do you want to try an increment?"

I nodded, feeling guilty again for taking up so much of eir time, but knowing ey'd reject the apology if I tried to give it. I could hear em in my head, saying "It's my time to spend how I like, darling," with that emphatic tone that showed love but no room for objection.

Ey twisted back my cutesy rainbow timer to 25 minutes, and stroked my hair as I turned back to write.

There were tears in my eyes, but so much love as well.

Delete, delete, delete went all the angry comments I'd left for myself. They were unworthy of the space that ey'd created here, devoid of joy or craft.

I typed out one sentence, then another. They weren't any good, or weren't as good as they could be, but it didn't matter. Soil-to-be, for more trees of words to grow.

I pulled up a set of photo notes, breathing calmly, finally, enjoying eir fingers on my scalp, eir arm on my side.

Another sentence, another. A paragraph heading. There was something brewing here, a novel little cool idea.

I popped up out of my seat, reaching for eir hand. A step back, a step forth. Rhythmic snaps from my off-hand.

"God, you're so cute when you're excited."

I smiled and spun to sit back down, typing out a pass at my little insight. Not its final form, but a shimmering little nugget that would make what I was creating just a little better.

Tears came to my eyes again. I am really something, I do have good ideas, I can create something.

I just  -- it's just hard for me, sometimes. I just need some help, some perspective, sometimes.

Sentences and sentences, and a timer finishing to the sounds of ocean waves and rain.

I leaned back, tension leaving me.

"I love you so much, sweetie."
