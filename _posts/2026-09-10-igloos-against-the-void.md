Now that I have a firmer understanding of what the void is,
and that I have a void problem,
I'm gonna lay out my current strategy
when I have a void urge or when I'm in the middle of voiding.
It's not perfect, it doesn't always work, but it's a start.

My strategy is summarized by the acronym IGLOOS, which stands for:

* Identify the void in progress (or the urge to void)
* Goal: Ten minutes without voiding
* Location change
* Obstacles to the void
* Open up to someone
* Storytelling: How will I feel in two hours, if I void?

And there's a bonus, final step, Forgiveness, which I'll get to after I dive into the first six.

And then comes progressing the task itself,
after the strategy is executed to leave the void.

Once again, if you think you might have a void problem, I'd really appreciate it if you get in touch. My contact info is at the bottom of the page. If nothing else, I'll feel less alone, and I'd like to learn about what all this looks like for you.

## Identify the void in progress (or the urge to void)

The first step of emerging from the void is identifying that we are in the void, currently.
This starts with something simple, like "I'm voiding right now".
That's why packaging up the void into a simple term is helpful,
it's a packaged idea that I can notice relatively easily.

The goal isn't moral judgement, not "I'm voiding, so I'm a piece of shit",
because that judgement triggers further avoidance which just continues the spiral.
Identification is just a neutral "This is what's happening now, this is void."

More detailed identification is fine, if we're up for that:
* "I'm avoiding right now."
* I'm doing diverting activities."
* "The reason I'm feeling a strong impulse to do that is because of the void." 
* "I'm feeling shame due to the void"

But the more detailed identification is optional. Just "That's some void shit" is fine.

## Goal: Ten minutes without voiding

The second step is to set our goal at a manageable level: go ten minutes without voiding, while maintaining mental engagement with the desired task.
And I mean literally ten minutes: set a timer.

Intentionally, the goal is not shooting for the stars.
We're not setting a goal of immediately making progress,
because that's void thinking:
Immediate progress as a coping mechanism for the FAHDSED complex of negative emotions (Fear, Anger, Hopelessness, Despair, Shame, Emptiness, Dissociation).
And we're not setting a goal of changing who we are as a person,
because that's just not going to happen:
we're literally talking about our goal for the next ten minutes.

But we're also setting a goal of ten minutes without void because it's a spiral.
It's self reinforcing.
Avoidance causes FAHDSED causes avoidance, around and around.
But those emotions fade, given space and time.
This goal is designed to give our spiraling emotions the space to settle,
rather than being churned continuously by the void.

You experience of the void fading might be different than mine.
I'm describing the goal that works for me, yours may need to be different.
As you try things, you'll learn what works for you.

When we're in the midst of the void, it can be hard to think of anything that's not voiding. So here's some possibilities for me:
* Stare blankly at the document associated with my current task
* Review notes about the current task
* Write down what the next increment of the task might look like, perhaps a 25 minute chunk
* Free write/journal about my feelings
* Talk aloud about my feelings
* Read this blog post
* Watch the gambling addiction
[urge video](https://youtu.be/rCLXCNWMz54?si=A16fLS8NdmC98E8D),
which this IGLOOS system is loosely based on.
* Change locations (see below)
* Open up (see below)
* Create obstacles to void (see below)
* Storytell about the future (see below)
* Drink water
* Eat food, if hungry

If your manageable non-void activities look different than might, consider writing your own list ahead of time.

But note, the goal here is to not void, but still be thinking about the task at some level.
We don't want to just go and do something else completely, like answer emails or whatever,
and then never return to our main task.
Because that's a short-term solution, but it doesn't address the core issue,
and it's very easy to end up just putting off the core task further.
So we're keeping the core task in mind, but allowing our emotions around it to fade.


## Location change

My experience of the void is very tied to the location in which I'm voiding.
This isn't to say that I only void in certain places,
but that the void spiral,
the pattern which maintains and reinforces the spiral,
is disrupted by changing locations.
Location changes also help me with creating obstacles to the void (see below).

Concretely, here's what this recently looked like on Friday.
I'd been voiding in my office for 3-4 hours.
After identifying the void and setting my goal,
I unplugged my laptop
and took my laptop, my timer, and my keys, and moving to an empty classroom.
And it was so much easier to stop voiding in that unfamiliar space than in my office
where I'd just voided for the last 3-4 hours.

I'm also taking a broad view of "location" here.
Changing which chair you're sitting in,
moving from a desk to a table,
from a computer to a pad of paper,
these are all significant changes that can make the void spiral less tight and consuming.

One caveat: The goal here is to change locations while maintaining engagement
with the overarching task,
not to give up completely.
I've definitely voided by going home under the void excuse that I just wanted dinner,
but really I was further fleeing from the emotions.
We don't want to do that.
So the location change is to make the emotions more manageable in this new context.
This is why we change locations after identifying the void and setting our goal.

## Obstacles to the Void

The void is maintained by how easy it is to do those avoiding, diverting activities
that continue the spiral.
This is why ADHD exacerbates the void, because it makes it yet easier to slip into those
diversions, but it's not essential to the void, because anyone can keep slipping into them.

To reach our goal of ten minutes without voiding,
we need to reintroduce some friction, slow down the shoot sliding us back into the void.

Here are some options:
* Close the browser tabs that we've been using to void - puzzles, engaging articles, videos, etc. Sure, we can reopen them, but that's friction, which is what we want.
* Put the phone out of reach and out of sight.
* Put away the e-reader
* Get up from the floor
* Engage a browser extension that blocks some of the common void paths - this needs to be set up ahead of time, but it might help
* Move to a social context where people are working.
For me, that's co-working arrangements,
but a coffeeshop or a library might work for you.

Note that these are tools to help in emerging from the void,
they do not replace take the control out of our hands.
Whatever obstacle we can create, we can also remove.
But they are friction.
They slow down the spiral, weaken its clutches,
and remove some of the pressure from our shaky willpower.
But in the end, the path out of the void is one we must walk.
The obstacles will not save us, on their own.

But we don't have to walk the path alone.

## Open up to someone

Out of the FAHDSED complex of emptions, a crucial component is shame.
The void thrives on shame, on making us embarrassed and guilty and ashamed and alone.
It wants us too embarrassed to admit to what we've done,
too embarrassed to tell anyone,
too embarrassed to admit it to ourself,
too ashamed to change.

And we need to emerge from the grids of that spiral, that shame.
And telling someone is a key step in that emergence.
That someone could be a friend, a relative, a group chat,
an acquaintance who happens to be nearby.
It can be a headmate, if you're plural and feeling spicy.
It can be me. My email's at the bottom

What do we tell them?
It could be "I'm voiding and it's really bad and I'm trying to emerge",
if they know what the void is and how consuming it is.
It could be something more detailed,
like "I'm really not doing well, I've been lying on the floor for hours avoiding work".
It can be tempting to say just something vague like "Today's not going well",
but it's important to keep going from there, to keep opening up.
We need connection, we need to let people in, to have that vulnerability,
in order to dissipate the cycle of emotion and avoidance.
Because once we tell people, that's something concrete and external
that more avoidance doesn't paper over,
and that helps bring us out of this purely internal mode of thought.

## Storytelling: How will I feel in two hours, if I void?

The void tries to tell us that voiding will solve all of our problems.
If we void, we'll feel calmer and soothed and then we can work effectively in half an hour
and then all of our problems will go away.

This is nonsense.
That's not how it actually goes at all.
When we void some, we void more, and more, and more.
It's a reinforcing spiral.

And we know it's nonsense: we can look back on the last time we voided.
But we expect things to go differently than they ever have in the past,
over and over and over again.
This is one of the elements of the void pattern that reminded me of addiction.

This is why it's key to telling the story of how the void will actually go for us,
drawing on that past experience.

For me, that might look like: If I void for half an hour, I'll probably end up voiding for much longer, two or four hours. And by the time I stop, I'll feel terrible. I really do care about this project and I'll feel horrible and incompetent and I'll just be thinking about all the time I wasted late into the evening. I don't want to feel that way. I don't want to void.

## Forgiveness

This is the special bonus seventh element of emerging from the void.

It's hard to emerge from the void.
We feel drained and exhausted, just from not voiding for 10 minutes.
It feels disproportionate,
like we're already failing just by feeling this strained and exhausted.
It feels unfair, other people don't have to go through this to get here.
Our brains are frantically generating reasons to go back into the void until the urge
starts to fade, just leaving us raw.
And we're feeling ashamed for the void we have done up to this point,
in this void period and the ones before.

I've always said that I feel the worst when I complete something, because that's when I'm most mentally engaged with this task I feel horrible about.

It's Ok to feel exhausted and drained.
Its not disproportionate, that's how the void leaves us feeling.
If it didn't feel horrible to emerge, it wouldn't be so consuming a spiral,
and we know all too well how consuming it is.

And we don't need to feel ashamed.
We're doing what we can to emerge from the void,
and we're doing enough.
We don't deserve to bear the guilt that lead us here.

Forgiveness can be hard.
There's a reason I placed it as the bonus seventh step.
Immediate forgiveness, before identification and goals and so forth,
doesn't work.
It just leads to sliding right back into the spiral.
That's not to say that we must earn forgiveness by struggle,
but that we weren't ready to think along those lines yet,
at that point in time.
Now, as we near our ten minute goal, we're ready to forgive ourself,
to feel our feelings that the void was trying to suppress, avoid, salve and hold in reserve.

## Moving into progressing work

Now, ideally, the void urge is fading, and has relaxed its grip on our mind.
Now, it's time to give work another shot.
This time, we're not working to relieve those emotions.
We are neutrally approaching work as just a think were doing,
relieved of its burdens of emotional baggage.

That work might not look like direct progress.
It might look like reaching out for support on things we're stuck on,
even to people we might be hesitant to bother.
That reaching out is hard, and it's work, and it's progress,
even if it's indirect progress.
It might look like setting up a co-working session.

It might look like flipping mindsets from
"I'm gonna prove this" to
"I'm gonna write down why this proof approach is completely flawed and will never work".
That's progress.

It might look like cracking off an approachable little piece of the larger whole, like making a little figure for a paper.

It might mean doing a pomodoro increment, specifying the goal for the next 25 minutes,
starting a timer, and poking away.

Ultimately, what this will look like will depend on your project, your goal.
But it's something you can approach with your whole mind, your thoughts and your emotions
unclouded (or less clouded) by the call of the void.
I believe in you.

## Conclusion

This isn't a one-size-fits-all approach.
I fully expect that what works for you will differ from what works for me.
But having a system, built up in advance and deployable when the void arises,
that's important and valuable.
And this system is open to adjustment and experimentation.
We can try building IGLOOS, see how it goes, see what works and what doesn't,
and adjust, and share our experiences, and learn.

I'm proud of you for trying.

Once again, if you resonate with the void experiences I've described,
