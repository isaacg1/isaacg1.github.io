## The problem
Recently, I've been trying to write a grant proposal. Each day. I was In my office from 10am till 8, 9, 10pm. Then I'd give up and go home. Those 12 hours, I barely progressed it at all. Maybe a couple of paragraphs. As I walked home, I'd still be thinking about it, about what I should've gotten done. No closer.

I gave up on the proposal. I'm not going to reach the deadline in time to submit something I'm proud of, not even close. Next year, I'll start in March. But will that really help? When I'm like this?

Hours upon hours upon hours lying on the floor "resting", "recovering".
Hours upon hours on my phone, watching videos, watching articles. Playing games, puzzles, chess, typing tests. Hours where the computer isn't on, or isn't on anything useful.  I'd briefly look at the task, then avoid, avoid, avoid. Progress for an hour at most out of ten hours spent.

Yesterday, I went home to pick up materials for a task around 4pm, told myself I'd come back to the office afterwards. I didn't. I justified my way into more couch time, more videos. I thought about going back and finishing it for 6 hours, went back to work at 10pm, and finished around midnight.

I'm not lazy. I do care. I care intensely and feverently and desparately. And yet.

## Admission

I have a problem. A severe problem. An unsustainable problem. A problem I am powerless to solve independently.

I cannot perform difficult solo work that I do not find intrinsically engaging, in a sustainable fashion.

I have tried everything internal that exists. Every mental framework, plan, course of action, theory, approach. Being kind to myself, being stern to myself. Analysis. Vibes. Splitting things into small tasks. Emotional states and management. Everything.

If you have a tip, a suggestion, a way of looking at things, I can assure you I've tried it, and it has ranged from useless to debilitating.

If I stay up till midnight, or two AM, to the point where I am truly exhausted and my sleep is disrupted, my mind often quiets enough that I can complete a bit of work. If I am desperately furious with myself to the point of tears, I can sometimes get something done.

But it's not reliable, and it's not sustainable, and it's no way to live. 

And sure, maybe you have something new. Some little tweak I've never tried. And maybe it's worked for you. Hooray, congratulations, I'm happy for you. Genuinely. But I can't believe it'll be any different, even as I try more and more and more. I have no hope left.

If I fill my life with only work that I do with other people, the problem vanishes. If a project can be completed only during time when I am working with someone else, there is no problem. Body doubling often helps too, even if the other person isn't involved. But I have to be talking with them, it doesn't work if we're separate. This is how I do my research now.

Working with others actively pulls me back from avoidance. I start avoiding during a long collaboration session, and the presence of someone else I care about pulls me back. It doesn't work if I don't care about them and the shared task, but it doesn't require intrinsic motivation.

This only carries me so far, and when I run into anything that must be solo, I collapse. I tell people I'll work on the project separately and I very very rarely do, and not reliably or sustainably. I let them down, again and again.

Obligations to others, like to my students or advisors (formerly) or advisees (now) can also activate this pattern, if the deadline is imminent enough. If I fill my life with tight, fine-grained deadlines to people I care about, that I will fail frequently. This is how I did my PhD, and this is how I teach.

If I fill my life with only work that I find intrinsically engaging, the problem vanishes. Programming queueing simulators and messing about with toy problems and trying out fun little proofs. I could do that all day everyday forever.

Intrinsic engagement pulls me back. I am currently finding this blog post intrinsically engaging, and that's the only reason I can write it. I feel the pressure inside myself to switch to my puzzle game, but the intrinsic engagement is holding me here, writing these words.

This only carries me so far, and when I run into something I care about that is not intrinsically engaging, I collapse. I need to formalize the proof or write the exposition or work on something else and I can't, not reliably or sustainably. I can't make things intrinsically engaging, no matter how desperately I want to complete them, no matter how many hours or approaches I pour into them. It's not something I can change, not reliably or sustainably.

If it's a minor task I can do with half my attention, I can coast through it with divided attention. This is how I do chores or emails.

But if it's solo, difficult, and not intrinsically engaging, I can't do it at all. And I need to stop pretending otherwise. 

## Emotions

I'm ashamed. I have so much I could be doing, so much privilege and time and freedom I've been handed, and all I'm doing is lying on the floor, playing a puzzle game and avoiding, at hours upon hours upon hours tick by. The same task I avoided all day yesterday, the same task I'll avoid tomorrow. The task I'm avoiding by writing this essay.

Sure, neurodivergence. Sure, radical acceptance. Sure, disability. Sure, comparing myself to a neurotypical version of myself. I know. But it doesn't change how I feel. I don't want to be this person, don't want to act like this, don't want to be like this. That's the reality of how I feel. I want it to be different. This is not part of my best self, this is not part of who I want to be.

I try to tell people, friends and partners and relatives and therapists. I don't think they really get it. I think they see a successful professor with a prestigious and steady job. They didn't see me crying on the floor again and again and again, they don't see the thoughts of "I can still get this done" ringing through my head as I walk home. They don't know me.

I feel despair, hopelessness. This will never change, and it will be the ruin of all my hopes and dreams, every worthwhile thing I attempt. As long as I live. 

I know there are options. I know that ADHD medication is powerful, that people find it very effective. But I'm scared, and I'm stubborn. I know myself. I know my mind. It's a wonderful mind and I love it very much and it and I have built a lovely live together. How can I now judge it unworthy, insufficiently effective, insufficiently optimized for capitalism, for writing grant proposals and other tasks I despise and would never touch if I had the option? How can I medicate it and change it, the foundation of my entire being? Will I lose myself to the seductive song of productivity?

I don't trust the beliefs I have on the matter of ADHD medication. I used to follow a blogger who is a psychiatrist, who believed that ADHD was merely a manifestation of normal people whose jobs depended on doing extremely boring things, trying to get an advantage, an upgraded stimulant. That ADHD meds were just coffee++. I don't trust anything this blogger said, I've rejected his views for a myriad of failings and am carving the influence he had on my out of my mind, one piece at a time.

I don't know if ADHD meds would lead to me losing a critical part of myself. I don't know if they would enable me to complete tasks that are difficult, intrinsically nonengaging, and solo. "DINS". But I'm terrified that if they are both, I wouldn't be able to stop. That I would become an emptier, blander version of myself if it meant a relief from this shame.

So I can't try them, while the seductive cry is so loud that it blinds me to everything else.

## Addiction 

I've been watching addiction recovery videos. Gambling, Kratom, others.

They feel relatable. Very relatable. Telling oneself that there's one new strategy that'll fix everything. That it's fine to engage in the behavior now that I have this great new framework. Friends and partners who know a little, who know the outlines of the problem but not the yawning void within. 

I recognize locking away temptations, banning certain apps and websites and actions, grayscale and time locked. It doesn't help, because the fundamental pattern is far more basic than any quick fix can solve.

But most of all, I recognize chasing excitement, being powerless before that hook, the dissociative powerlessness like it's not even me making choices,  like I can't do anything else. Like this is all I know.

I haven't reached rock bottom, like these people have. I don't know if it'll look anything like what they've been through.

I know what worked for them. Community. Other people in the same hole, working towards the same way out.

That's what I need: community. I need a community of people for whom ADHD, and the particular manifestations in takes in their lives, is a problem. I know that's not everyone's experience, but it is my experience. 

I think it's time to find a group therapy place. I think that's my next step.

## Being ADHD isn't the problem

I don't want to stop being ADHD. It is a key part of myself and I wouldn't have it any other way. I want to stop this endless avoiding. I do think they are separable. I think my quick shifts of topic and long intense engagement with my interests are separable from this pit of avoidance. ADHD is who I am, this avoidance is not. I don't have a good name for it. I've heard it called "ADHD task avoidance", but that feels so minor and anodyne, and all I see in response are more fucking helpful little tweaks that will never change anything.

I know I need to forgive myself. I know I need to start medication. I know I need to form community. I know I need to keep adding more tweaks until I reach a successful whole. I know, I know, I know. And yet.

Avoid. A void. That's what it feels like. A void of time and emotion and purpose and effort and tweaks and strategies, sucking at my self and my purpose and my substance, endlessly sucking away. "The void". That's what I'll call it to myself, a name proportionate to the severity of the problem. I lost ten hours to the void, yesterday. Yeah. That sounds right.

## Me and the Void

Where I'm going from here:
* Self-forgiveness. Being sucked into the void whenever I work on DINS tasks is normal, expected, unexceptional and not worthy of blame. Of course I'm sucked into the void, it's massive and sucking and hostile. Of course other people aren't, they don't have a void following them around. Of course other tasks don't lead to this, I can hold on to them and not be sucked in.
* Support group/group therapy. I reached out to one place, I'll find one that works.
* Trying more tweaks. Sure, they won't make a big difference on their own, but if I don't put my hopes and faith into them, they can help a bit sometimes. To-do lists and post-it notes and free-writing, they've all helped a bit. And they give me a sense of purpose, even if it will be sucked into the void before long.
* Find more people's stories about medication. I need to do more to overwrite the malign influence of that one fucker. Maybe I end up on "take as needed" medication, who knows. We'll see.

That's what I've got. This is who I am. This is where I'm at. I'm cried empty.
